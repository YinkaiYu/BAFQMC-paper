# Reference Verification

Use this before adding or modifying any BibTeX entry. For literature discovery,
sentence-level evidence audits, potential-referee coverage, PDF caching, and HTML
reports, also read `citation-research-and-reporting.md`.

## Required Metadata

Verify each field against arXiv, APS, DOI/Crossref, or publisher pages:

- author list
- title
- journal
- volume
- page or article number
- year
- DOI
- arXiv identifier, if cited or relevant

Do not infer metadata from memory.

## Citation Strategy

- Match each source to the exact clause it supports. Topic overlap is insufficient.
- Distinguish the role of each source: original method, theorem, physical discovery,
  application, review, textbook background, limitation, or recent development.
- Prefer original and authoritative sources for specific claims. Reviews are useful
  for field maps and citation chains, but they do not automatically replace the
  underlying paper.
- Broad clusters are welcome for established background, major applications, and
  community coverage. A cluster must remain semantically coherent.
- Place citations close to the supported claim. In a list of physical examples,
  cite each example separately.
- Do not retain a prestigious or author-strategic paper when it does not support the
  sentence. Move it to a better claim or omit it.
- Distinguish work that solves a sign problem, mitigates it, studies its complexity
  or physical meaning, applies an already sign-free method, or motivates a target
  phase.

## Claim-Level Check

For every citation in changed prose, answer:

1. What exact words does this paper support?
2. Is it direct evidence or only contextual evidence?
3. Is there an earlier, more authoritative, or more precise source?
4. Does the sentence imply more than the paper establishes?
5. Would moving the citation to another clause improve attribution?

For mature text, adding a newly discovered paper does not by itself justify adding a
new sentence. Preserve the story unless the evidence changes the scientific claim.

## Reporting To Author

After adding or changing references, report:

- citation keys added or edited
- source used for verification
- any uncertainty or metadata mismatch
- whether the exact BibTeX-key PDF is present in the local cache

Do not disguise missing metadata or a missing PDF with a guessed entry or an
unrelated replacement. Mark the gap explicitly for author review.
