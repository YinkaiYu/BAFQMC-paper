# Research Note Writing

These instructions apply to research notes in this directory. Read them together
with the repository's root `AGENTS.md`. They set the presentation style for notes
when general manuscript guidance emphasizes PRL compression or exhaustive formal
proofs. Preserve archived documents unless an edit to them is requested.

## Audience And Purpose

- Write for collaborators and a broad audience of physicists. Assume readers have
  none of the authors' private discussions or collaboration history.
- Give each note a clear scientific question and keep its scope focused. Explain
  why the question matters and what the result lets a physicist understand or do.
  Include background when it helps the reader follow that question.
- Present the science directly. Keep responses to author requests, revision
  announcements, and sentences tailored to private feedback out of the note.

## Exposition And Physical Picture

- Develop the argument like a lecture, making the mathematical reasoning and
  physical picture clear. Either may lead, or they may develop together. Show the
  derivation steps needed for understanding rather than replacing them with
  qualitative discussion. Explain the meaning, assumptions, and purpose of
  mathematical terms and results as they become necessary.
- When introducing a new condition, transformation, or calculation, make its
  purpose clear. Explain what physical question it resolves and connect the result
  to an intuitive physical picture when one is available.
- Let each paragraph use what has already been established and prepare the next
  step. Supply the logical bridge between equations and claims. Avoid abrupt
  changes of subject and catalogues of specialist terminology.
- Organize derivations around the clearest route to the result. A record of
  intermediate attempts and historical notation usually adds reading burden.
- Keep conclusions and direct criteria usable on their own when that is their
  scientific purpose. State the object being tested, conventions, assumptions,
  and conditions needed to apply them.

## Derivations And Notation

- Let the physical purpose guide the mathematics. Prefer elementary, intuitive
  derivations with explicit steps; explain any abstract tools needed and how they
  help answer the physical question.
- Aim for correct, concise, physically transparent arguments. Full formal rigor
  and exhaustive treatment of mathematical edge cases are unnecessary when they
  do not affect the physical result or its use.
- Work through the essential algebra. Show the nontrivial steps a physicist needs
  to check the result, with a short explanation of why each step is useful.
  General statements in prose cannot substitute for a needed derivation.
- Retain assumptions that change a claim's validity or applicability. Check signs,
  conventions, and limiting cases at the depth needed to support the result.
- Reuse established notation. Introduce a variable, symbol, definition, or acronym
  only when it materially simplifies the argument or is needed repeatedly. Prefer
  a direct expression to a short-lived auxiliary object.
- Define unfamiliar terms and symbols where they first become necessary. Use
  familiar physics language and explain any indispensable mathematical term.
- Avoid invented concepts, private abbreviations, internal labels, and chains of
  technical names. Check terminology throughout the note for hidden reading costs.

## Scientific Voice

- State supported results confidently and explain their physical significance.
  Keep qualifications that affect the result. Remove defensive weakening and
  disclaimers that add no scientific information.
- State the intended point positively and directly. Avoid `not ... but ...`,
  `不是……而是……`, and similar negation followed by correction.
- Prefer connected sentences and paragraphs. Use semicolons and colon-led
  constructions sparingly. Lists should help readers follow steps or compare
  genuinely parallel conditions.
- Keep the note's voice independent of the author-assistant exchange. A reader
  should understand every sentence from the scientific context on the page.

## References And Final Read-Through

- Verify every new or modified bibliography entry against authoritative publisher,
  DOI, or arXiv records. Check authors, title, journal, volume, page or article
  number, year, DOI, and arXiv identifier where applicable. Never guess metadata.
- Check that each citation supports its nearby claim. Reference accuracy is an
  academic-integrity requirement.
- Read the revised argument in order as an unfamiliar physicist. Check that the
  motivation and physical picture are clear, essential derivations are present,
  and the reader can apply the result without private context.
- Remove unnecessary terminology, symbols, detours, defensive phrasing, and
  responses to private requests. Preserve scientific correctness while reducing
  the reader's work.
