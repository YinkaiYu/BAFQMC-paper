We now solve the generator equations for every semigroup class.  A representative built from the matrices in Eq.~\eqref{eq:real_pauli_basis} may be tensored with an arbitrary site or orbital space.  Its complete solution is
\begin{equation}
    A_{\rm group}=\sum_P P\otimes K_P,
    \qquad
    A_{\rm cone}=\sum_P P\otimes K_P,
    \label{eq:pauli_generator_expansion}
\end{equation}
where each displayed \(P\) is a Pauli tensor and \(K_P\) acts on the additional space.  The first factor in \(P\) acts on the Nambu components and is denoted by the superscript \({\rm N}\) in each representative.  Further factors act on physical internal components and carry \({\rm f}\), or \({\rm f}_1,{\rm f}_2\) when two internal factors are needed.  Every generator list states both equations obeyed by each \(K_P\).  The inequality printed for each class acts on the complete sum in \(A_{\rm cone}\) and can couple several coefficient matrices.  The universal point
\begin{equation}
    A_{\rm cone}=-\eta,
    \qquad
    \eta A_{\rm cone}=-I<0
    \label{eq:negative_direction}
\end{equation}
belongs to every cone listed below.  The identity on the additional space is implicit.

The first Pauli factor has a direct operator meaning.  Combining the internal factors and \(K_P\) into a matrix \(K\), \(I_2^{({\rm N})}\) and \(\sigma_z^{({\rm N})}\) give number-conserving terms,
\begin{align}
I_2^{({\rm N})}:&\quad \sum_{ij}\hat a_i^+K_{ij}\hat a_j,
\qquad K^\trans=-K,
\label{eq:I_operator_term}\\
\sigma_z^{({\rm N})}:&\quad
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \sum_{ij}b_i^+K_{ij}b_j+\frac12\Tr K,&\text{bosons},\\[3pt]
\displaystyle \sum_{ij}c_i^+K_{ij}c_j-\frac12\Tr K,&\text{fermions},
\end{cases}
\qquad K^\trans=K.
\label{eq:sigmaz_operator_term}
\end{align}
The factors \(\sigma_x^{({\rm N})}\) and \((\ii\sigma_y)^{({\rm N})}\) change particle number by two,
\begin{align}
\sigma_x^{({\rm N})}:&\quad
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \frac12\sum_{ij}K_{ij}(b_i^+b_j^+-b_ib_j),&K^\trans=K,\\[3pt]
\displaystyle \frac12\sum_{ij}K_{ij}(c_ic_j+c_i^+c_j^+),&K^\trans=-K,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:sigmax_operator_term}\\
(\ii\sigma_y)^{({\rm N})}:&\quad
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \frac12\sum_{ij}K_{ij}(b_i^+b_j^++b_ib_j),&K^\trans=K,\\[3pt]
\displaystyle \frac12\sum_{ij}K_{ij}(-c_ic_j+c_i^+c_j^+),&K^\trans=-K.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:isigmay_operator_term}
\end{align}
The upper and lower lines in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:sigmax_operator_term} and \eqref{eq:isigmay_operator_term} are the bosonic and fermionic expressions.  Internal Pauli factors describe flavor-dependent hopping, conversion, and pair creation or annihilation.

The condition is
\((-\sigma_x^{({\rm N})}\otimes\sigma_y^{({\rm f}_1)}\otimes\sigma_x^{({\rm f}_2)})A_{\rm cone}<0\).  Setting
\(K_{\sigma_x\otimes\ii\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_x}=-\ii I\) and all other cone coefficients to zero gives \(A_{\rm cone}=-\eta\).  Both generator spaces contain number-conserving and fermion-pairing terms.  Four annihilation modes are required because two anticommuting operations with square \(-I\) need two independent internal doublets.  Their four creation partners give eight Nambu components.

Across all sixteen classes, \(A_{\rm group}\) satisfies \(\eta A_{\rm group}+A_{\rm group}^\dagger\eta=0\) and can carry auxiliary fields of either sign.  The complete \(A_{\rm cone}\) must satisfy its displayed matrix inequality.  The listed directions are fixed-field quadratic slice terms.  Individual slices may be non-Hermitian even when the original interacting Hamiltonian is Hermitian, as is common after a Hubbard--Stratonovich transformation.
